Two ways in.
One belongs in production.

Both pipelines put the same origin behind the same playback CDN. They differ only in how the encoder reaches it — and that one difference decides who is allowed to write to your live stream.

01 · Topology

The ingest CDN adds a hop and buys no fan-out

A CDN earns its keep by serving many readers from one origin fetch. Ingest has exactly one writer. Putting CloudFront in front of it adds a hop, a TLS terminate and re-originate, and an edge function invocation on every manifest — with nothing to fan out.

02 · Who can write — the decisive difference

An ingest CDN means any distribution can PUT

The origin has no authentication. Access is controlled entirely by the security group. Put CloudFront in front of ingest and that group must accept PUT, POST and DELETE from CloudFront’s entire origin-facing prefix list — because that is the only source address CloudFront presents.

Which means any CloudFront distribution in the world, in anyone’s account, is inside your write perimeter.

direct-ingest restricts writes to EncoderCidr, and the template refuses 0.0.0.0/0 outright.
Playback stays on the prefix list either way — but limited to GET, HEAD and OPTIONS.

The honest counterweight

Direct ingest is plaintext HTTP. The feed is readable in transit and injectable by anyone who can reach that address. TLS on the origin needs a real domain and a publicly trusted certificate, because CloudFront rejects a self-signed certificate on a custom origin — worth doing, and separate work.

If the encoder is on-premises, use Direct Connect or a VPN. Do not push a live feed across the open internet because a template made it easy.

03 · Latency — unresolved

Does the ingest CDN batch the chunks?

Removing the edge function and the POP hop is worth tens of milliseconds against a 3–4 second target. Nobody perceives that. Only one question could actually matter: whether the ingest CDN coalesces the encoder’s 0.2 s fragments into larger writes.

pass-through0.2 s fragments arrive as sent
segment startsthe reader is never far behind the writer2.0 s
coalescedbatched into ~0.5 s writes
segment startsthe reader waits for each batch — up to ~0.5 s of added latency2.0 s
NOT MEASURED. An earlier observation saw the origin receiving ~51 KB every ~0.5 s through the ingest CDN — but that 0.5 s may have been the sampling interval, not CloudFront’s behaviour. It was never isolated.

To settle it: run both pipelines from one encoder and sample a single segment’s size every 20 ms at each origin. Steps mean coalescing; a smooth ramp means pass-through. Do not ship a latency claim without this.

04 · Playback

One caches at the edge; the other hits the origin every time

This is a deliberate difference, not an oversight. The testing pipeline disables playback caching so that every request is observable at the origin — correct for measurement, wrong for scale.

↓

Manifest TTL is 1 s in production, at or below the encoder’s minimumUpdatePeriod: a stale manifest moves the player’s idea of the live edge.
403, 404 and 5xx are never cached — a segment asked for a moment early exists a moment later.
The 6 h segment TTL is safe only while segment names carry a per-run token. Recycled names would serve stale video.

06 · Verdict

Use direct-ingest. Keep the other for two jobs.

direct-ingest — production

Fewer moving parts, and writes restricted to one address instead of a global prefix list. Deploy one template, fill in one field. The origin adds UTCTiming itself, so nothing is needed on the encoder.

Its real cost is plaintext ingest. Treat that as a networking decision, not a checkbox.

cdn-ingest-testing — keep, do not deploy for production

It is the only way to answer the coalescing question, and the only way to exercise the Lambda@Edge manifest path. Its origin is built from the same tag as production, so the ingest path is the only variable — it previously cloned upstream, which made the origin a variable too, and an unpatched one.

Measured on the deployed origins, not estimated. Latency figures are equivalent between pipelines; the coalescing question is explicitly unresolved.
